# Reflexive-closure bounds for ordinary semisimple restrictions

October21,2026. AI-SEMISIMPLE-REFLEXIVE-027. Written, unreviewed.
All fields in this argument are C. The operator-space theorem is external.
No historical priority is claimed for that theorem or its proof.

Let E be a codimension-at-most-k subspace of Mat(d). Write R(E) for the
linear span of its rank-one matrices and L=E^perp under the entrywise
bilinear pairing. Then

    R(E)^perp = Ref(L) = {B: B*x lies in L*x for every x}.

Indeed u*x^t belongs to E precisely when u annihilates L*x. Apply the same
condition to B and use finite-dimensional double annihilation.

Ambrozie, Kuzma and Muller, *An upper bound on the dimension of the
reflexivity closure*, Proc. AMS138(2010),1721-1731,
[Theorem2.7 and its proof, equations(2.1)-(2.4)](https://www.imar.ro/~purice/Inst/2013/CAmbrozie/proc10184.pdf),
prove dim Ref(L)<=h*(h+1)/2 for h=dim L. Their evaluation-kernel proof also
bounds its s-th successive evaluation image by h-s+1. Each image has
dimension at most d. Summing over a basis of the d-dimensional domain gives

    dim Ref(L) <= C(d,k)=sum_{s=1}^d min(d,max(k-s+1,0)),
    dim R(E) >= W(d,k)=d^2-C(d,k).                        (1)

Here h<=k and the displayed bound is monotone in k. This finite-domain
refinement is read directly from that credited proof; it is not a new
reflexivity theorem.

## Semisimple application

For a source S=direct_sum_i M(d_i) of dimension N, and an ordinary
restriction to a rectangular target direct_sum_j M(a_j,b_j,c_j), put
M=sum_j a_j*b_j and k=N-M. Intersect the first input image with each
source Mat(d_i) block. Each intersection has codimension at most k.
By(1), at least W(d_i,k) independent parameters in that block are spanned
by rank-one matrices, whose source slices have rank d_i. Different source
blocks have disjoint parameters. Slice compression does not increase rank.
The target's rank<=r parameter locus spans exactly its blocks with c_j<=r.
Therefore

    sum_{i:d_i<=r} W(d_i,k) <= sum_{j:c_j<=r} a_j*b_j.     (2)

Apply this in each cyclic ordering of the legs. This strengthens the
column argument in claim024, whose lower bound was only
sum_i d_i*max(d_i-k,0).

## Exact boundary controls

The bound W(7,5)=34 is sharp. Take L generated by the first five powers
of a single nilpotent Jordan block on a fixed five-dimensional coordinate
subspace, and zero on its complement. For any vector with last nonzero
coordinate t in that subspace, L*x is the first t-coordinate space.
Thus Ref(L) is exactly the upper triangular5-by5 block, of dimension15.
The rank-one span in L^perp has dimension49-15=34.

The bound W(7,10)=6 is also sharp. Let E consist of matrices whose
anti-diagonal sums of degrees3 through12 vanish, interpreting entries as
coefficients of u(t)*v(t). These ten constraints are independent, so E
has dimension39. A rank-one element u*v^t has product polynomial of degree
at most2. Over C the degree of a product is the sum of the degrees, so
all its entries have i+j<=2. Those six matrix units themselves lie in E;
its rank-one span therefore has dimension exactly6. This explains why
replacing W(7,10) by the full intersection dimension39 would be wrong.

The tests independently form the Jordan and Hankel constraint spaces and
check their ranks and explicit rank-one spanning controls. No finite test
is substituted for the arbitrary-subspace theorem.
