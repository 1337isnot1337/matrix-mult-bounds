# An explicit first polynomial flag and its target action

Date: October 21, 2026.

Status: an exact partial construction. It satisfies every tensor equation
involving target input degrees zero and one. It does not complete the higher
input degrees and does not imply a new matrix multiplication exponent.

## 1. Parameters and the first three input rows

Let `m>=4`, `N=m(m+1)/2`, and `B>=N`. Put `R=B+m`, the output
dimension of `C(m+1,B)`. Polynomials of degree at most `d` are denoted
by `P_d`. Choose linearly independent shared forms
`u_0,...,u_(m-1)` in `P_m`, and set `U=span(u_i)`.

Suppose `u_0,u_1` are coprime as binary forms of degree `m`.
Choose `h in P_(B-m-1)` and `w_0 in P_(B-1)`. Define

`w_y=h u_1`, `w_x=h u_0`, and `W_1=span(w_0,w_y,w_x)`.

The following two explicit conditions suffice:

- `h` is nonzero and `U w_0` has dimension `m`;
- `U w_0` and `h U span(u_0,u_1)` have zero intersection.

The only relation among the `2m` products in
`U span(u_0,u_1)` is the commutation relation. Indeed,
`f u_0+g u_1=0`, with `f,g in U`, implies
`f=c u_1`, `g=-c u_0` by coprimality and the degree bound.
Consequently

`dim(U W_1)=m+(2m-1)=3m-1`,

and the kernel of the product map `U tensor W_1 -> P_(B+m-1)`
is exactly the line generated by

`u_0 tensor w_y-u_1 tensor w_x`.

For the target fat-point tensor, the same vector is a relation:
the coefficient of `X_0 Y_(0,1)` equals the coefficient of
`X_1 Y_(1,0)`, both in the output coordinate `Z_(0,m-2)`.
No assertion about a generic coefficient tensor is used here.

## 2. Exact output maps for these rows

The target outputs in degrees `m-2` and `m-1` number `2m-1`.
On `U W_1`, prescribe their values by the target coefficient rule

`T[X_i,Y_(a,b),Z_(c,d)]=1` exactly when
`a+b+c+d=m-1` and `i=a+c`.

The sole product relation just proved is also a target relation.
These prescriptions therefore define linear functionals on `U W_1`;
extend them to `P_(B+m-1)` by ordinary linear algebra.

All lower target output rows are chosen in
`E=ann(U W_1)`. Its dimension is

`dim(E)=B-2m+1 >= (m-2)(m-1)/2`.

The right-hand side is exactly the number of target output monomials
of degree at most `m-3`. Thus these rows can be independent. The high
output rows are independent modulo `E`, because the target restriction
to the first three input rows has output rank `2m-1`. The resulting
output map has full target rank `N`.

Assign input weights `w_Y(p)=max(0,p-1)` and output weights
`w_Z(q)=-w_Y(m-1-q)`, with shared weight zero. For input degrees zero
and one, all lower-output coefficients vanish exactly, and all constant
coefficients equal the target. In fact the unweighted coefficients on
these first three input rows already equal the entire corresponding
target sub-tensor. The higher input rows can be completed to an
independent set, but their negative and constant coefficients remain
unsolved.

## 3. Explicit integer choices prove nonemptiness

There is no numerical genericity assumption. Take distinct exponents

`d_0=0`, `d_1=m`, and
`(d_2,...,d_(m-1))=(1,3,4,...,m-1)`.

Thus the exponent `2` is omitted. Set

`u_i=t^(d_i)`, `h=t^H`, `H=B-m-1`, and `w_0=1`.

For `m>=4` and `B>=N`, one has `H>m`. The low product exponents
`{d_i}` are therefore disjoint from the high product exponents

`{H+d_i} union {B-1+d_i}`.

The two high sets intersect only at `B-1`, since the only two chosen
exponents whose difference is `m` are `0,m`. They have union size
`2m-1`. This proves both required product-space conditions over the
integers. The same full-rank conditions are Zariski open, so the
construction also holds for a nonempty open set of the stated data.

The high output rows can be written explicitly. Let `e_k` be the
coefficient functional at `t^k`.

- For output total degree `m-1` and first exponent `c`, take
  `e_(d_c)`, for `0<=c<m`.
- For output total degree `m-2` and first exponent `c`, take the
  sum of `e_(B-1+d_c)` and `e_(H+d_(c+1))`, for `0<=c<m-1`.
  When `c=0` the two positions coincide, so include that coordinate
  only once.
- For lower output rows, use distinct coordinate functionals outside
  all the product exponent sets.

Every map entry in this example is zero or one. The accompanying
verifier checks the coefficient identities directly, without floating
point arithmetic.

## 4. What a recursive construction must still do

After this first step, the remaining multiplication tensor is induced
on `P_(B-1)/W_1` and `ann(U W_1)`, of dimensions `B-3` and
`B-2m+1`. Its filtered target action remains to be constructed.
An arbitrary basis of these spaces does not supply the higher
catalecticant blocks.

The common-factor presentation has a useful Hilbert--Burch form.
When `w_0=a u_0+b u_1`, the two polynomial syzygies are

`(0,u_0,-u_1)` and `(h,-b,-a)`.

They have coefficient degrees `m` and `B-m-1`, respectively, when
the forms have the stated exact degrees. This is a concrete starting
point for a quotient or Bezoutian recursion, not a proof that such a
recursion exists. In particular, the sparse integer first-step examples
need not have the full Kronecker type required by the next block.
