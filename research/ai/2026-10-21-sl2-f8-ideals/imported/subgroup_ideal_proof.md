# Full subgroup cosets force a smaller output space

Status: written, unreviewed. This is a local application of elementary
subgroup averaging and the two-sided ideal generated by its idempotent.
It is not an exponent improvement or an exclusion of arbitrary complex maps.

Let G be a finite group and H a subgroup. Over C put

    e_H = (1/|H|) sum_{h in H} h,
    I_H = C[G] e_H C[G],      D_H = dim_C I_H.

Suppose injective pair maps and strict vertex potentials construct the
matrix tensor for vertex sets (S,T,U), and T contains a complete left
coset gH. Then

    |S|*|U| <= D_H.                                      (1)

The analogous statements hold for either other vertex set. The same
dimension applies to every conjugate of H.

## Proof

First restrict T to gH. Every intended term of this smaller rectangular
matrix tensor remains, and all its extra support terms are among the
original extra terms. The pair maps stay injective and the original strict
potentials remain feasible.

The action t->t*h for fixed h in H permutes this restricted vertex set and
preserves all support equations: the occurrences t*h and (t'*h)^-1 in the
adjacent pair labels cancel their inserted h and h^-1. Average the feasible
potentials over this finite permutation action. Intended terms still have
weight zero and every extra term still has strictly positive weight, since
it is an average of strictly positive weights. The T-potential is now a
single constant, beta.

Write the first two coordinate embeddings as

    A_(s,t) -> epsilon^(alpha_s-beta) * s^-1*t,
    B_(t,u) -> epsilon^(beta-gamma_u) * t^-1*u.

The output coordinate rescaling is the usual one determined by the third
potential. Restrict the middle target index to its one-dimensional trivial
H-isotypic part, using a constant average on one input and a constant column
on the other. Because beta is constant, these restrictions commute with
the displayed epsilon weights. On the group side the first input lies in
C[G] e_H and the second in e_H C[G], after harmless nonzero normalization
factors. Their product lies in I_H for every nonzero epsilon.

On the target side the restriction is M(|S|,1,|U|), whose output flattening
has rank |S|*|U|: its distinct outer products span every matrix unit. The
source output flattening has rank at most D_H before the limit, even after
the output rescaling and restriction maps. Matrix rank at most D_H is a
closed condition, defined by minors, so the limit has the same upper bound.
This proves (1). Cyclic symmetry handles the other two legs. Conjugation
is an algebra automorphism carrying I_H to the corresponding ideal, and
therefore preserves its dimension.

No claim about an arbitrary degeneration follows: the averaging and the
commutation with the diagonal epsilon weights use the coordinate/vertex
potential construction explicitly. Characteristic zero is used to divide
by |H| and to preserve positivity while averaging integer inequalities.
Rational averaged potentials can be cleared to integers.

## Exact finite ideal certificates

The ideal I_H is spanned by g*e_H*h. Ignoring the common nonzero scalar
1/|H|, these are precisely indicator vectors of all left cosets of all
conjugates of H: gHh=(gh)(h^-1 H h). This gives a rational matrix whose
rank over Q equals its rank over C, with no character-table assumptions.

For an explicitly generated cyclic subgroup of order five, the exported
certificate gives:

| Group | Group order | Conjugate subgroups | Coset vectors | D_H |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| SL(2,5) | 120 | 6 | 144 | 80 |
| PSL(2,5) x C2 | 120 | 6 | 144 | 88 |
| SL(2,5) x C2 | 240 | 6 | 288 | 160 |

An integer annihilator family of size |G|-D_H supplies the exact upper
rank bound. Its values at the listed free coordinates form a nonsingular
diagonal matrix, proving independence. Every vector annihilates every
coset row by direct integer summation. A separate elimination modulo the
prime1,000,003 gives rank D_H, proving the matching lower bound. The latter
is only a rank lower bound over Q; the upper bound comes from the exact
integer annihilators, so no bad-prime inference is used.

It follows that no leg of an M10 coordinate/potential target in either
order120 group may contain any full order-five coset: the other pair has
size100, greater than80 and88 respectively. For the order240 target
M(13,13,15), all three other-pair products exceed160, so the same conclusion
holds on every leg. These are necessary cuts; the remaining target sets
are still open.

The finite implementation also checks a sharp positive boundary in C3^2:
for a line subgroup H, D_H=3, and the ordinary coordinate target
M(3,1,3) with two distinct lines has the required other-pair size3. The
cut respects this actual witness. A corrupted annihilator is rejected.
