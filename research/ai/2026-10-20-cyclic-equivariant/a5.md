# A size-five Cartan frontier and its remaining constructive target

October 20, 2026. AI-CYCLIC-CARTAN-SCREENS-052. Written proof with
exact finite enumeration, unreviewed. **No new exponent is proved.**

The earlier constant-map screens in
[AI-CYCLIC-FINITE-TARGET-041 through 043](../2026-10-20-koszul/cyclic_finite_target.md)
left four improving shapes at a=5. The later
[full low-rank-locus theorem](../2026-10-20-anisotropic-constructions/polynomial_rank_locus.md)
excludes all four for ordinary maps. Retaining the specified diagonal
Cartan action closes three of them, even for parameter-dependent
intertwining maps. The remaining M(14,14,15) has explicit compatible
characters but no exhibited tensor maps.

## 1. A full 225-dimensional leg forces the other vertex to have dimension at most 11

For A5 each leg has dimension 225 and character

    S=t^-8 P, P=(1+t+t^2+t^3+t^4)^2(1+t+...+t^8).

The coefficient vector at weights -8,...,8 is

    1,3,6,10,15,19,22,24,25,24,22,19,15,10,6,3,1.

Consider a matrix target M(l,15,15) with torus vertex characters U,V,W.
Conciseness implies coefficientwise target-leg character bounds as in
[the H1100 character proof](torus.md). The 225-dimensional leg fills
the source character exactly: V W*=S. The exhaustive nonnegative
factor search gives only two ordered factorizations, interchanged by
swapping the two vertices. Their normalized coefficient vectors are

    v=(1,2,3,3,3,2,1),
    w=(1,1,1,2,2,1,2,2,1,1,1).

The integer coefficient recursion from `torus.py` checks 16111 nodes;
it does not assume irreducibility from a symbolic factorizer. Equivalently,
these are (1+...+t^4)(1+t+t^2) and (1+...+t^4)(1+t^3+t^6).

Translate so W has weights 0 through 10. Then V has weights 2 through 8.
The support condition U W* <= S forces U to lie at weights 2 through 8.
Write its multiplicities as u0,...,u6, allowing zeros. Four edge
coefficients of U W* and the central coefficient of U V* give

    u0 <= 1,
    u0+u1 <= 3,
    u5+u6 <= 3,
    u6 <= 1,
    u0+2u1+3u2+3u3+3u4+2u5+u6 <= 25.

Adding the five inequalities yields 3 sum(u_i) <= 33, hence l<=11.
The reciprocal character and the pairwise inequalities are symmetric
under swapping V,W, so this covers both ordered factorizations.

Thus A5 cannot degenerate to M(l,15,15), l>=12, within this Cartan
intertwining class. In particular it excludes M(13,15,15) and
M(14,15,15) here. General symmetry-breaking degenerations remain open.

## 2. A 224-dimensional leg cannot factor into vertices of dimensions 14 and 16

For a target leg of dimension 224, its character must be S-t^j for
some integer -8<=j<=8. Consequently the normalized polynomial is
P-t^k, 0<=k<=16, after removal of endpoint zeros and the associated
monomial shift. Exhaustive nonnegative coefficient factorization
with factor sums 14 and 16 gives no pair for any of the 17 cases.
The search visits 250611 nodes; the complete per-case report is saved.
An independent SymPy factorization discovery check agreed, but the
acceptance calculation uses the integer coefficient search.

No torus-intertwining degeneration can therefore produce a matrix
tensor with a 14-by-16 leg. This excludes M(13,14,16) within the class,
without any assumption on the maps' parameter orders.

## 3. The remaining target and the useful smaller member

For a=3,4,5 put D=a-1 and choose vertex characters at weights 0,...,2D:

    U=V=(1,1,2,...,2,1,1),
    W=(1,2,...,2,1,2,...,2,1),

where U has 2a-5 twos and W has a-2 twos on each side of its central
one. Their dimensions are 4a-6,4a-6,4a-5. All three pair characters
are coefficientwise at most the source character in these three cases.
After centering and restoring standard SL2 weights, the formal identities
are U=chi(V_(2D))+chi(V_(2D-4)) and
W=chi(V_(2D))+chi(V_(2D-2))-chi(V0).

The family includes A3 to M(6,6,7), H1100 at a=4, and A5 to M(14,14,15).
At a=5 the explicit vectors are

    U=V=(1,1,2,2,2,2,2,1,1),
    W=(1,2,2,2,1,2,2,2,1).

These are necessary character data, not a construction or a complete
classification of every compatible size-five character triple.

A simple ordinary slice check helps distinguish the cases. The unique
source A-coordinate of weight 2D has i=s=0 and k=2D. In its B,C block
at weights (-D,-D), the only terms are

    B_(j,D,j) C_(j,D,j), j=0,...,D.

Its matrix rank is a. The selected target slice has rank one, from
the central weight of W. If each of the other two weight spaces loses
delta dimensions, any ordinary compression has rank at least a-2delta.

| a | Source B/C weight-space dimension | Target B/C dimension | Rank lower bound | Target rank |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 3 | 6 | 5 | 1 | 1 |
| 4 | 10 | 9 | 2 | 1 |
| 5 | 15 | 13 | 1 | 1 |

Thus the a=4 ordinary obstruction remains, while a=3 and a=5 pass
this particular test at equality. Passing does not establish sufficiency.

The existing cyclic-source implication gives, conditional on an actual
A5 to M(14,14,15) degeneration,

    omega <= 9 log(28)/log(216*2940) < 449/200 = 2.245.

The strict rational comparison is exactly

    635040^449 > 28^1800.

This is a conditional consequence of the earlier source/profile theorem,
not a new exponent result. The smaller a=3 member alone would not improve
9/4 by that implication. It is a possible construction guide, not a
sufficient bound certificate.
